Commander Basics

Commander is the most played way to enjoy Magic with friends: a 100-card deck, one legendary leader, and three or more players at the table.

What makes Commander different

  • Your deck has exactly 100 cards, counting your commander.
  • It is singleton: only one copy of each card, except basic lands. A few cards, like Relentless Rats, say you can have any number of them.
  • One legendary creature leads the deck. It is your commander, and it starts the game face up in the command zone instead of in your library.
  • Everyone starts at 40 life, and games usually have four players, each playing for themselves.

Choosing a commander

Your commander has to be a legendary creature, or a card whose text says it can be your commander. The Wandering Minstrel is a legendary creature, so it qualifies. Some commanders have partner or a similar ability that lets two of them share the role.

Pick a commander you want to see every game. You can cast it again and again, so its abilities shape how the whole deck plays. The Wandering Minstrel cares about Towns, so a deck built around it wants plenty of Town lands.

Color identity

Every card in your deck has to fit inside your commander's color identity. That identity is every color in the commander's mana cost and every colored mana symbol in its rules text. Reminder text doesn't count.

The Wandering Minstrel shows why both parts matter. It costs only green and blue, but its last ability costs {3}{W}{U}{B}{R}{G}, which uses all five colors. So its color identity is all five colors, and a deck it leads can play cards of any color.

Lands follow the same rule. A land that taps for red has red in its identity and can't go in a deck whose commander is only green and blue.

The command zone and commander tax

Your commander waits in the command zone, a spot beside the battlefield. You can cast it from there whenever you could cast it from your hand.

Each time it would go to your graveyard, hand, library or exile, you can move it back to the command zone instead. Casting it again costs {2} more for each time you have already cast it from the command zone. This extra cost is the commander tax. The Wandering Minstrel costs two mana the first time, four the second, and six the third.

Commander damage

A player who takes 21 or more combat damage from the same commander over the course of the game loses, no matter how much life they have left. Damage from different commanders is counted separately, and only combat damage counts.

This is why a big commander is a threat even at a full table: it can knock out a player at 40 life in a few hits. The combat basics guide covers how attacking and blocking work, and melee shows a keyword built for games with several opponents.

Playing with several opponents

  • Each attacking creature attacks one opponent, planeswalker or battle of your choice, so you can split your attacks.
  • Your first mulligan is free: you draw a new seven cards without putting any on the bottom.
  • Talk about power level before you start. Many groups use the official brackets, from 1 for casual decks to 5 for the most competitive ones, so everyone brings decks of a similar strength.
  • A banned list applies to Commander. Check it before you finish a deck.

Does the printing matter?

No. The surge foil above plays exactly like the plain version of The Wandering Minstrel. Special frames, foils and alternate art only change the price. Look up any commander in the card search to see every printing and pick the one that fits your budget.
