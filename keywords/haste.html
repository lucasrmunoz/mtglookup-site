Haste

A creature with haste can attack and use tap abilities the turn it comes under your control.

Keyword glossary › Haste

What it does

Normally a creature can't attack, or use an ability with the tap symbol in its cost, unless you have controlled it continuously since the start of your most recent turn. Players call this “summoning sickness”.

Haste removes that wait. A creature with haste can attack the turn you cast it, and it can use its tap abilities right away.

Haste only matters on the turn a creature comes under your control. After that, every creature you have kept since the start of your turn can attack anyway.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking summoning sickness stops blocking. Any untapped creature can block, even one that just entered.
  • Thinking it lets a creature attack twice or attack while tapped. Haste only removes the wait.
  • Forgetting control matters, not when it was cast. A creature you take from an opponent needs haste to attack that turn.
  • Expecting a token to dodge summoning sickness. Tokens need haste to attack the turn they appear, too.

Interactions worth knowing

  • Vigilance plus hastelets a new creature attack and still be ready to block on your opponent's turn.
  • Effects that steal a creature until end of turn, like Threaten, usually give it haste so you can attack with it right away.
  • A mana creature with haste can tap for mana the turn it enters, because haste covers every ability with the tap symbol, not just attacking.
  • Losing haste after attackers are declared doesn't remove a creature from combat. The check happens only when attackers are declared.
