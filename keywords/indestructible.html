Indestructible

A permanent with indestructible can't be destroyed, neither by lethal damage nor by effects that say "destroy".

Keyword glossary › Indestructible

What it does

A creature normally dies when it has damage equal to or greater than its toughness: the game destroys it. Indestructible switches that off. The damage is still dealt and still marked on the creature, but it isn't destroyed, and the damage wears off at the end of the turn as usual.

Spells and abilities that say “destroy”, from a single-target kill spell to a “destroy all creatures” board wipe, also do nothing to it.

Indestructible is not the same as “can't leave the battlefield”. A creature with 0 or less toughness is put into its owner's graveyard, and exile, sacrifice and bounce effects all work normally, because none of them destroy anything.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking it can't be damaged. It takes damage like anything else; the damage just doesn't destroy it.
  • Expecting it to survive −X/−X. Zero toughness sends it to the graveyard without destroying it.
  • Expecting it to dodge sacrifice or exile. Only destruction is stopped.

Interactions worth knowing

  • Deathtouch destroys the creatures it damages, so it does nothing against indestructible.
  • Trample still only has to assign lethal damage to an indestructible blocker. Lethal is based on toughness, so a trampler blocked by an indestructible 3/3 sends everything past 3 to the player.
  • An indestructible blocker can block the biggest attacker every turn and never die, which makes it a great wall.
  • Board wipes that say “destroy all” leave indestructible creatures behind.
