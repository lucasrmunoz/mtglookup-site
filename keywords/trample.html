Trample

If a creature with trample is blocked, it can deal the damage left over after killing its blockers to the player or planeswalker it attacked.

Keyword glossary › Trample

What it does

Without trample, a blocked attacker deals all of its damage to the creatures blocking it, even if a blocker has 1 toughness and the attacker has 10 power. The extra damage is wasted. Trample fixes that.

When a creature with trample is blocked, its controller assigns lethal damage to each blocker first. “Lethal” means enough to destroy it, counting damage already marked on it this turn. Whatever power is left over can be assigned to the player or planeswalker being attacked.

If a trampler is blocked and every blocker then leaves the battlefield before damage, all of its damage goes to the player. A creature without trample in the same spot deals no damage at all.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping a blocker. Every blocker must be assigned at least lethal damage before any goes to the player.
  • Expecting trample to help on defense. It only matters when the creature attacks and is blocked.
  • Thinking trample is what lets an unblocked creature hit the player. Every unblocked creature does that.

Interactions worth knowing

  • Deathtouch makes 1 damage lethal, so a deathtouch trampler assigns 1 to each blocker and the rest goes through.
  • Chump blocking a trampler with a small creature barely helps the defender, as the example shows.
  • Pumping a trampler after blockers are declared sends all the extra power to the player.
  • Trample is mostly green and red, and shows up on most of the big creatures in those colors.
