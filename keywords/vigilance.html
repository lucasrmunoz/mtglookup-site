Vigilance

Attacking doesn't cause a creature with vigilance to tap.

Keyword glossary › Vigilance

What it does

When a creature attacks, it normally taps. A tapped creature can't block, and it stays tapped until your next untap step, so attacking usually leaves you with one less blocker on your opponent's turn.

Vigilance removes that cost. The creature attacks without tapping, so it is still ready to block on your opponent's turn. It can also still use its tap abilities after combat.

Vigilance changes nothing else about combat. The creature deals and takes damage the same way, and it still needs to be untapped to attack in the first place.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking it protects the attacker. A vigilance creature can be blocked and destroyed like any other.
  • Thinking it untaps a creature. A tapped creature with vigilance still can't attack.
  • Forgetting it can tap after combat. An untapped attacker with a tap ability can use it once combat is over.

Interactions worth knowing

  • Haste plus vigilance lets a creature attack the turn it arrives and still guard you on the next turn.
  • Lifelink plus vigilance gains life on offense and can block to gain more on defense.
  • Effects that tap a creature, like Coeurl's ability, still work on a vigilance creature. Vigilance only stops attacking from tapping it.
  • Abilities that trigger when a creature becomes tapped don't trigger when a vigilance creature attacks, because it never taps.
